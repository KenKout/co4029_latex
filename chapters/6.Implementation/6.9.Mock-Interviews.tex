\subsection{Mock Interview Sessions}
\label{sec:impl_interviews}

The mock interview subsystem evaluates whether a learner can explain and apply material through a controlled conversation rather than a form submission. Its implementation is in \path{abridgeai/features/interviews/}, with authoring and publication services, a persisted session runtime, an adaptive orchestrator, a LiveKit integration, and asynchronous evaluation workers. The boundaries between those parts are deliberate: authoring decides what may be asked, the runtime records what was asked and answered, and the evaluation worker decides what evidence the completed session demonstrates.

\paragraph{Authoring, publication, and reusable questions}
Teachers create interview configurations as drafts, with course learning outcomes seeded into the interview rubric. A configuration cannot be published without at least one approved question and one defined outcome. Questions may be written manually, generated from selected course materials, or copied from the course-scoped question bank. AI-generated questions enter a review lifecycle; manually authored questions are approved directly but remain subject to the same published-configuration freeze. The question bank is a reusable source rather than a shared live object: importing an item copies it into a configuration, so later bank edits do not silently alter an already configured interview. Duplicate detection is advisory, using semantic similarity to flag a possible overlap for the teacher rather than blocking authoring automatically.

Publication freezes the conduct, grading, retake, outcome, and question content that determines an assessment. This prevents a teacher edit from changing the rules of a live or completed session. Approved questions may optionally link to one learning outcome; the readiness checks report unlinked questions or incomplete outcome coverage, but these diagnostics do not replace the teacher's publication decision.

\paragraph{Persisted onboarding and hybrid session entry}
A new learner session is persisted as \texttt{in\_progress} with \texttt{input\_mode = hybrid}. The client does not choose separate text and voice session types: typed interaction and optional microphone interaction share one hybrid room. The session first advances through the persisted onboarding stages \texttt{identity\_check}, \texttt{audio\_check}, \texttt{language\_check}, \texttt{preparation}, and \texttt{readiness}. Each onboarding response is idempotent and is retained as a non-question-linked transcript turn. The assessment clock begins only after readiness completes, and the first assessed question is withheld until then. This keeps setup interaction and equipment problems outside the assessable evidence.

Session creation is also idempotent. A retry after a lost response returns the original session, while a learner who already has an active session resumes it rather than consuming another attempt. A fresh attempt is checked against the teacher-configured maximum-attempt and cooldown rules. Completed, timed-out, and abandoned sessions consume the attempt policy; a system or evaluation failure remains auditable but does not consume an allowance or start a cooldown.

\paragraph{Hybrid transport and deferred native analysis}
The frontend holds the LiveKit room above the text and voice workspace views so changing interaction surface does not reconnect the session. Voice-token issuance and agent dispatch are deployment-gated, and agent dispatch is withheld until onboarding completes. Typed learner turns and microphone input are both associated with the persisted session-question ledger and transcript.

The native voice bridge can use a fast sufficiency probe to support immediate progression. A later \texttt{arq} reconciliation task performs fuller answer analysis and updates provisional runtime coverage with optimistic-lock retries. Reconciliation improves subsequent question selection but is not the final assessment: terminal evaluation independently judges the durable transcript and question ledger.

\paragraph{Perception, decision, and safe fallback}
The adaptive runtime separates perception from decision. The perception pipeline classifies learner intent and extracts structured answer evidence; the decision layer selects a follow-up, hint, next question, or closing action. Adaptive runtime state is persisted separately with a version counter and turn idempotency key. Replayed turns return their existing result rather than rerunning model calls, and competing state updates surface a conflict instead of silently overwriting a previous decision.

Adaptive behaviour is controlled by a master switch, per-mode and sub-feature flags, and a stable per-student rollout percentage. When enabled, the adaptive branch executes in a savepoint after the learner answer has been recorded once. If the adaptive pipeline, state write, or output guard cannot complete, its partial writes roll back and the established sequential progression path advances the same recorded answer. Shadow mode can compute adaptive decisions for observation without allowing them to affect learner-facing progression. Thus a model-side failure degrades the interaction quality rather than losing a learner response or ending the assessment.

Question selection tracks phase progression, answer sufficiency, outcome coverage, and difficulty. Follow-ups and hints are bounded by teacher-configured limits so one topic cannot expand indefinitely at the expense of other outcomes. Interviewer persona --- strict, neutral, or supportive, with warmth, directness, verbosity, and formality dimensions --- changes phrasing rather than the assessment standard. Persona adherence is checked independently of outcome assessment.

\paragraph{Security, integrity, and recording boundaries}
The runtime screens learner input for prompt injection, protected-content extraction, and related manipulation attempts, and screens generated output before it reaches the learner. The guard defaults to shadow mode: it assesses and records redacted signals without changing the learner-facing flow. Explicit enforcement configuration may warn, refuse, redirect, or end the relevant turn according to the configured response policy; a guard event does not itself determine the interview verdict.

While a session is active, the browser reports focus loss, tab switches, fullscreen exits, reconnects, and disconnects. A policy snapshot taken at session start fixes the weights and threshold used for the server-side integrity score; tab switches, focus loss, and fullscreen exits contribute to that score. Crossing the threshold creates a one-time warning and flag while the interview continues. These events are review signals for authorised teachers, not proof of misconduct or an automated disciplinary outcome.

Audio recording is a separate optional capability. It requires both deployment enablement and affirmative consent for the current recording-policy version. Stored answer-audio links and an optional audio-only session recording support later authorised review, but recording provider, webhook, storage, or reconciliation failures are handled best-effort and never terminate an interview or alter its evaluation.

\paragraph{Terminal evaluation, recovery, and result visibility}
A session that reaches assessment and is completed, or times out after answer evidence exists, dispatches asynchronous evaluation through \texttt{arq}. A session abandoned before assessment is never graded. The worker evaluates outcome evidence, derives the pass verdict from the configured outcome rule, persists diagnostic rubric information, and generates a \texttt{GapReport}. The rubric is diagnostic; it is not an independent learner-facing pass gate.

Evaluation is protected by a durable exclusive claim. Only the worker holding that claim can publish \texttt{InterviewOutcomeEvaluation} rows, the session verdict, and the gap report, preventing a retry or recovery worker from making completion order determine the recorded result. Transient worker failures use bounded retries, while lifecycle recovery can redrive sessions whose dispatch or worker state was stranded. A terminal evaluation failure remains visible as an unavailable result state rather than being replaced with an invented verdict.

Learner and teacher projections deliberately differ. After successful evaluation, learners receive the binary pass/fail verdict and qualitative remediation or study plan, without numeric rubric scores, per-outcome verdicts, or internal reasoning. Teachers with course-scoped authority can review the transcript, outcome evidence, rubric diagnostics, integrity timeline, recording metadata where available, and teacher notes. The separation preserves useful remediation while preventing assessment internals from becoming answer keys.

\paragraph{Stage and status are different axes}
Two vocabularies describe a session and must not be conflated. The onboarding \emph{stage} records how far a learner progressed through the checks; the session \emph{status} --- \texttt{in\_progress}, \texttt{completed}, \texttt{timed\_out}, \texttt{abandoned}, or \texttt{failed} --- records how the session as a whole ended. A session can be abandoned during \texttt{audio\_check} as readily as mid-answer, so the stage and status are stored separately. Only answers recorded after onboarding completes form assessable evidence; completed sessions and eligible timed-out sessions may be evaluated, while an abandoned pre-assessment session is not graded.
