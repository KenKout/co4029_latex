\subsection{Course and Curriculum}
\label{sec:impl_course}

Courses are the unit a learner enrols in and the container every other feature hangs from. The implementation lives in \path{abridgeai/features/courses/}, split by audience rather than by entity: \path{authoring.py} for the teacher's editing surface, \path{catalog.py} for the learner's browse and detail views, \path{assignment.py} for teacher--course bindings, and \path{administration.py} for the operator actions that cross both.

\paragraph{The curriculum tree}
A course owns ordered modules, and each module owns ordered items --- lessons, quizzes, and interview configurations addressed uniformly so the learner's next step is a single ordered walk rather than three parallel lists. Ordering is a dense \texttt{position} integer per parent under a unique constraint, so two items can never claim the same slot; reordering rewrites the affected range in one statement rather than issuing a move per row.

\paragraph{Publication gating}
A course, like every other authored artefact in the system, moves through \texttt{draft}, \texttt{published}, and \texttt{archived}. Draft content is invisible to learners at the query level rather than filtered in the presentation layer, so an unpublished lesson cannot leak through an endpoint that forgot to check.

Neither transition is reversible, and the asymmetry is deliberate. Publication is a one-way door: a published course can never return to draft, because its learning outcomes double as the graded assessment scale and re-opening it for editing would move the goalposts under students already enrolled against them. Archival is the separate terminal state, reached from either of the others, and it withdraws a course from new enrolment without disturbing the enrolments already running through it.

\paragraph{What publication requires}
Three conditions gate the transition into \texttt{published}, each refusing a course that would fail a student in a different way. At least one gradeable unit --- a published lesson, quiz or interview --- because completion is computed from units, and a course with none can never be completed: as a required course on a career path that locks its stage, and every stage behind it, permanently. At least one teacher, measured against a per-organisation minimum, so a live course is staffed. At least one learning outcome, because a course that never states what it teaches cannot be assessed against anything. All three are evaluated only on the way in, so re-publishing a course that is already live does not re-litigate a rule an administrator has tightened since.

Publication also announces itself: the teachers attached to the course and the students already enrolled in it are notified once, on that same first transition. Dispatch is per recipient and best-effort --- a failure is logged and the publication stands, because a message that could not be delivered is not a reason to withdraw a course that is otherwise fit to be live.

\paragraph{One transition, two doors}
A course reaches \texttt{published} either through the dedicated publish endpoint or by patching its status, and for a time the two did not agree. The patch applied the gradeable-unit gate alone --- it skipped the staffing and outcome gates, and it notified nobody --- so the same course was publishable or not depending only on which endpoint the caller chose. Nothing surfaced the divergence, because each door was internally consistent. The announcement compounded it: since notification fires only on the transition, a course published through the patch could never be announced afterwards, the dedicated route finding it already published and staying silent in turn.

The patch no longer re-implements the transition; it delegates to it, applying the rest of the payload first so the announcement describes the course as patched rather than as it was a moment earlier. Copying the missing gates across instead would have produced two implementations that must be kept in step by hand, which is how they came apart in the first place. The same permission now guards both doors, so they cannot differ on who may open them either, and a refused publish rolls back the whole patch rather than leaving half of it applied.

Archival has the same shape and took the same fix. Archiving a course that sits on a published career path is refused, because withdrawing it would lock that stage --- and every stage behind it --- for every enrolled student; writing the status column straight from a patch walked past that check. Both lifecycle moves are now reached through one implementation each, whichever endpoint is called.

\paragraph{Deletion is for drafts; archival ends a published course}
Deleting a course is a draft-only operation, for every role including an administrator. Nothing can yet depend on a draft --- enrolment refuses a course that is not published, and a published pathway cannot hold an unpublished course --- so removing one costs nobody anything. Once a course is published, archival is the only way to end its life. A draft deleted in error is recovered by restoring the soft-deleted row rather than by a state change --- an operator action with its own audit trail, not an ordinary edit.

The asymmetry exists because the soft-delete cascade cannot reach far enough. It walks the course's own children, the modules and lessons and items beneath it, but a pathway's reference to a course is a row in a join table that carries no deletion marker and no relationship back to the course. The cascade therefore leaves it standing, pointing at a course that progress queries no longer return: the stage quietly loses a requirement, its remaining work becomes the whole of it, and it can complete --- and unlock the stage after it --- under students who are mid-pathway. That is the same harm archival is written to prevent, which is why the more destructive door must not be the less guarded one.

\paragraph{When a live course may be archived}
Archival is refused while the course still sits on a pathway somebody could reach. Establishing that is subtler than it first appears, because publishing a pathway does not retire the version before it (Section~\ref{sec:impl_career_paths}): a course attached once is recorded in that version permanently.

Asking only whether the pathway is published therefore blocks archival forever. Detaching the course in a new draft and republishing does not help --- detachment is a draft-only edit, so the earlier published version keeps its row, and the pathway is still published. A course could be taken out of a live curriculum months earlier and remain unarchivable, which left an operator holding only the deletion route, the one door that does real damage.

A version is treated as reachable when new enrolments would land on it --- it is the current published version of a still-published pathway --- or when somebody is still walking it, pinned there either by a career enrolment or by a programme path attempt. A version that is neither is history: the course is recorded in it and nothing reads it. So the exit is the ordinary authoring one. Fork a draft, remove the course, publish; the old version stops being the one new students pin to, and once its last student has finished or moved on, the course archives.

\paragraph{Syllabus import}
\path{syllabus_import.py} accepts a structured syllabus and materialises an entire module tree in one operation. It follows the same per-row discipline as the programme roster import: a malformed entry is reported against its position and the remainder still imports, because a syllabus with one bad row is the normal case rather than the exception.

\paragraph{Completion is counted over the visible curriculum}
The course's own completion is an aggregate over its units, and the population that aggregate runs on has to be the same one the learner is shown --- not merely similar to it. Visibility is decided at two levels, because the curriculum screen builds itself from published modules and then from the published items inside them. A unit therefore counts only when both hold: a published, undeleted lesson, quiz or interview, sitting in a published, undeleted module.

The second condition is easy to omit and expensive to omit, because the failure is silent and asymmetric. Filtering the item but not its container admits a published lesson inside a draft or archived module: absent from the learner's screen, present in the denominator. The student cannot reach it, so the tally can never close, the enrolment never promotes to completed, and the career-path stage behind it stays locked --- for every student on that course, indefinitely, with nothing in the interface suggesting why. Archiving a module is an ordinary editorial act, which is what makes the omission reachable in normal use rather than only in a contrived one.

The same population is used by the teacher's roster, the progress summaries and the readiness figures, for the same reason the completion rules have a single implementation: a course that reads complete to the learner and incomplete to their teacher is a defect in whichever of the two computed it alone.

\paragraph{Enrolment and entitlement}
Learners reach a course three ways --- operator enrolment, a career path or programme that grants it, and self-enrolment by invitation code --- and all three converge on one enrolment row whose status is \texttt{active}, \texttt{completed}, \texttt{dropped}, or \texttt{waitlisted}. Keeping the grant paths separate from the enrolment record is what allows a programme path change to release an entitlement without disturbing an enrolment the learner obtained independently.
