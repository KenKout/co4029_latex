\subsection{Notifications and Discussions}
\label{sec:impl_notifications}

\paragraph{Notifications}
Dispatch is implemented in \path{abridgeai/features/notifications/}. A notification carries a category drawn from a fixed vocabulary --- course updates and announcements, material processing, quiz and interview generation outcomes, path-change review, lesson discussion, and system messages --- and learners hold preferences per category rather than per event, so a single toggle governs a whole class of message instead of accumulating switches.

Message text is built in \path{messages.py}, which holds one function per notification moment and renders English or Vietnamese from the recipient's own locale. Keeping the copy in typed builders rather than templates is what makes a new locale a compile-time obligation instead of a silent fallback to English.

Delivery tracks \texttt{pending}, \texttt{sent}, \texttt{failed}, and \texttt{cancelled}, with e-mail dispatched by a background worker so the request that triggered it never waits. Crucially, a notification failure is swallowed and logged rather than propagated: the write that caused it --- a dean's approval, a completed import --- is an academic record change, and losing it because an inbox row could not be written would be far worse than a missing notification.

\paragraph{Discussions}
\path{abridgeai/features/discussions/} implements threaded discussion on lessons and courses: topics carry comments, comments carry direct replies, and both are soft-deleted so a removed post leaves the thread's shape intact rather than orphaning the replies beneath it. Authorship and edit windows are enforced in the service layer, and a new comment raises a \texttt{course\_discussion} notification through the same category machinery as everything else.

\paragraph{The dispatch path}
A domain event reaches a recipient through a fixed sequence. The event resolves to a category; the category is tested against that recipient's stored preference for the channel in question, and a pair with no stored preference falls back to a declared default rather than to silence; the recipient's locale selects the builder; and the builder produces the text. From there the two channels diverge. The in-app row is written inside the triggering transaction, so an inbox entry and the change it reports commit together or not at all. E-mail is handed to a background worker instead, because an outbound SMTP call is slow and may fail for reasons that have nothing to do with the academic record being written.

That divergence is where the swallowed failure sits. The boundary is drawn immediately around dispatch: anything that goes wrong beyond it --- a worker error, a rejected address, an unreachable relay --- is logged and dropped, and can move the delivery row to \texttt{failed}, but can never roll back the dean's approval or the completed import that triggered it. The states \texttt{pending}, \texttt{sent}, \texttt{failed} and \texttt{cancelled} therefore describe the fate of the message alone, never the fate of the transaction behind it.
