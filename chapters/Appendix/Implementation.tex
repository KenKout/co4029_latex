\paragraph{Where completion is written}
Three of the lifecycles above complete as one cascade rather than independently, and the table cannot show that because the transition crosses all three. The evaluation described in Section~\ref{sec:pathway_completion} is performed once, against the career-path version in question; where it holds, the learner's career enrolment is marked \texttt{completed}, every programme path attempt on that path whose \emph{own} pinned version also evaluates complete is closed as \texttt{completed}, and a programme enrolment left with no attempt still \texttt{active} is marked \texttt{completed} with the date recorded. The middle step is qualified deliberately: two programmes may pin different versions of the same path, and a result earned against one version must not close an attempt that promised another.

The cascade is not driven by the reported progress percentage. That figure is produced by whichever formula the deployment-wide setting selects, and under the legacy formula a pathway satisfying every required course and its full elective quota still reports below $100$. Gating these writes on it would leave all three lifecycles open indefinitely; they are gated on the stage evaluation itself, as Section~\ref{sec:impl_path_governance} sets out.


\subsection{Cross-cutting implementation notes}

\paragraph{Soft deletion and referential policy}
Entities that carry academic meaning are soft-deleted rather than removed, and any parent carrying the soft-delete mixin declares \texttt{ON DELETE NO ACTION} on its children. Hard deletion therefore cannot happen by cascade; it happens only where a test or a maintenance job walks the live foreign-key graph explicitly. The effect is that a mistaken delete fails loudly against a constraint rather than silently removing a subtree of results.

\paragraph{Feature independence}
Cross-feature calls are confined to each feature's \path{api/public.py}, and the restriction is machine-checked by import-linter contracts in continuous integration rather than left to review. A feature that reaches into another's internals fails the build, which is what keeps the boundaries in the previous sections real rather than aspirational.

\paragraph{Concurrency}
Decisions that must not race --- a path request being approved, an interview being evaluated, a grade of record being recomputed --- take a row lock or a reservation before reading the state they will act on, and re-verify their preconditions inside it. Optimistic locking is used instead where a retry is cheap and a conflict is expected to be rare, notably on interview turn state, where a lost race surfaces as an explicit stale-state error rather than an overwrite.

\subsection{The learner journey across subsystems}

The preceding sections each describe one feature package. Read end to end, they compose into a single path that is worth tracing once, because the handoffs between packages are where the design decisions actually bind.

A learner enters through a programme enrolment, which pins a programme version and places them on its default career path. That path entitles a set of courses; entitlement is recorded separately from enrolment, so a later path change can withdraw access without disturbing a course the learner reached by another route. Within a course, modules hold lessons, quizzes and interviews as sibling items in one ordered walk. A lesson's materials have already passed through ingestion --- extracted, chunked, embedded and indexed --- and that derived corpus is what the generation pipelines retrieve from when a teacher drafts a quiz or an interview question bank, every draft of which waits for human approval before any learner sees it.

The learner then attempts the quiz. Grading produces a grade of record; the same responses seed spaced-repetition cards, whose ease factors and intervals evolve on a schedule of their own. The unlock rule reads both: prerequisite completion, the quiz result, and the proportion of the lesson's cards standing above the configured ease floor. Passing it opens the next lesson, and where the module requires it, the mock interview probes whether the learner can explain what the quiz established they can recognise. Completion propagates upward --- units satisfy a course, courses satisfy a career-path stage, stages satisfy the pathway, and the pathway completes the programme enrolment the learner started from.

Two properties hold along the whole of that path and are easier to see here than in any one section. Nothing generated by a model reaches a learner without a human approving it, and every rule that judges a learner is frozen at the moment it starts applying to them --- the integrity policy onto the attempt, the pathway version onto the enrolment, the programme version onto the student --- so that editing the curriculum never retroactively changes what someone already under way is being measured against.

\subsection{Unlock evaluation in detail}

Whether a lesson opens for a particular learner is decided by three inputs of different kinds, and the distinction between them matters when a result looks wrong.

The first two are per-lesson configuration: which lesson, if any, is a prerequisite, and the standard the learner must reach --- the minimum ease factor, the proportion of the lesson's cards that must stand above it, and whether a passing interview is also required. The third is per-learner state: the learner's actual card ease factors, their quiz grade, and their interview verdicts. Configuration is authored once and applies to a cohort; state is personal and changes with every review.

The evaluation is deliberately conservative about missing data. A prerequisite on which the learner has no card history at all is not treated as failed but as unevaluated, and the distinction is surfaced rather than collapsed: a learner who has never attempted the prerequisite needs a different intervention from one who attempted it and fell short. Collapsing the two would present an untouched lesson and a failed one identically, which is the most common way a gating rule becomes untrustworthy to the teacher relying on it.

\subsection{Work performed outside the request path}

Several operations are too slow, too failure-prone, or too unrelated to the caller to run inside an HTTP request, and are dispatched to the background worker pool instead: material ingestion, quiz and interview generation, notification e-mail, audit writes, and the retention sweeps that expire old records.

They differ in how failure is handled, and the differences are deliberate rather than incidental. Ingestion and generation failures are \emph{reported}: the job's status is recorded against the entity and surfaced to the teacher who started it, because a teacher who uploaded a document is waiting on an outcome and silence would read as success. Audit and notification failures are \emph{swallowed}: they are logged, but they never propagate into the transaction that triggered them, because losing a dean's approval to a failed inbox write would be far worse than losing the notification. Retention sweeps are \emph{retried}, being idempotent by construction --- deleting what is already deleted is a no-op --- so a transient database error simply means the sweep runs again on its next schedule.

\subsection{Deployment topology}

The deployed system is a single-page application served to the browser, a FastAPI service handling requests, and a worker pool consuming the job queue, over four stores: PostgreSQL with \texttt{pgvector} for relational and vector data, Redis for caching, rate limiting and the queue itself, an S3-compatible object store for uploaded media, and Neo4j for the course knowledge graph. External model providers sit outside all of it, reached only through the LLM gateway.

Two boundaries in that picture carry more weight than the rest. The first is the trust boundary crossed by learner-supplied content: uploaded material and interview utterances originate outside the system and are treated as untrusted wherever they meet a model prompt, which is what the interview input and output guards exist to enforce. The second is the object store path: clients upload and download through presigned URLs issued by the API rather than streaming bytes through it, so the API handles the authorisation decision while the store handles the transfer. That split is why material size is bounded by storage policy rather than by request timeouts.
